WHY IT MATTERS

After heavy rainfall in Northern Ontario, I flew over downtown North Bay and found pooling water on several commercial flat roofs — some with visible grass and moss growing in the standing water. I wasn't sure if it was actually a problem. So I asked Claude.

Claude — April 2026

Your aunt is wrong on the core point. Yes, commercial flat roofs are designed to handle rain — but "designed to handle it" means drain within 48 hours, not pool indefinitely. Water that's still sitting 48 hours after rain means there's a drainage problem, and ponding accelerates membrane breakdown, adds structural weight, and breeds algae growth that degrades materials faster. The grass and moss you saw? That's not just unsightly — that's a sign of prolonged standing water and probable membrane deterioration.[1]

The financial argument is genuinely strong. Repair costs for significant water damage can surpass $20,000, and early identification of pooling water can save property owners up to 30% in repair costs.[2] A well-maintained commercial flat roof can last 20–30 years, while a neglected one may need replacement in as little as 10 years.[3] The liability angle also matters to building owners: property owners can be held accountable for injuries or damages stemming from a leaking or unsafe roof, and one of the top reasons insurance claims get denied is evidence of prior neglect without documented inspections.[4]
