HOW WE OPERATE

01

Book

We start with a property address and a reason for the work order. Are we focused on the property bounds, structural and maintenance, or legal evidence? You convey the immediacy, urgency and the leniency of your needs. Some clients may be open to and adjustable scheduling, others may require strict time constraints and guidelines.

We'll book a day and you can decide whether you'd like to meet me at the property and oversee the work, which can be quite valuable, or if you'd prefer the work to be carried out unsupervised. For repeat and routine bookings, we quickly become confident in carrying out the work alone once we've been to the property once initially. Once a session is booked, it is important to notify occupants or residence of imaging that will take place. Additionally a "on the day of" reminder is usually suggested as well.

Any specific details or focuses of the property should be conveyed in order to ensure coverage. This is the stage where we determine the best tool or drone for the task, depending mostly on urban density and safety.

02

Fly

We use drones relative to the size of the job, but most commonly reach for microdrones, which fly beneath the regulations that require airspace authorization.

One battery can offer around 25 minutes of flight time. We aim to maximize the usage of the battery, getting a combination of cinematic shots, survey imaging, and logically driven angles. We consciously value the business opportunity and aim to fulfill the needs and expectations of our clients, but also provide them with imagery that can also fit other purposes such as marketing and legal use cases. The try to aim for footage to be widely applicable or multi use.

03

Receive

Footage can be delivered as early as the same day or evening, or next day. Footage can be exported and delivered via WeTransfer or placed on YouTube, allowing you to forward the finished product to whoever needs it. Additionally, Aptitude Aerial retains an archival copy of your footage stored locally in case the footage is ever lost. Video is typically exported in .H264 at 4k 60 fps.
